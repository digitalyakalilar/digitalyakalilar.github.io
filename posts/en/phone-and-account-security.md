Your phone knows everything about you: your emails, messages, banking app and work accounts. But is it protected well enough? Take 5 minutes and work through these 6 steps.

## 1. Turn on two-factor authentication
Two-factor authentication protects your **accounts**, not your phone: even if your password is stolen, nobody can get into your account. Turn it on for email, Instagram, WhatsApp and LinkedIn. If you can, use an authenticator app instead of SMS.

## 2. Don't reuse the same password
If one site is breached, every account that shares that password is at risk. Use a separate, strong password for each account, and let a password manager remember them for you.

## 3. Watch out for fake links and texts
Don't tap links in messages that rush you, like "Your package is waiting" or "Your account has been suspended." When in doubt, open the app or website yourself and check.

## 4. Don't put off updates
Updates don't just bring new features; they also close security holes. Turn on automatic updates for your system and apps.

## 5. Review app permissions
A flashlight app doesn't need your contacts or location. Turn off unnecessary permissions under Settings > Privacy.

## 6. Screen lock and "Find My Device"
Use a strong screen lock (a PIN of at least 6 digits, or biometrics). Turn on "Find My Device" on Android or "Find My" on iPhone, so you can lock and erase your phone remotely if it's lost.
