Readiness is not installing a tool. Readiness is writing down who can change the decision that tool makes.

The **Türkiye CHRO Agenda 2026** study, by PERYÖN, FutureBright and Elkin, measured that. Ninety-two CHROs answered, mostly from organizations with more than 1,000 employees. Five CEOs and six CHROs were interviewed. The results were published on 11 November 2026 [1].

Seventy-nine percent say their agenda has grown. Five percent call themselves very ready. Fifty percent call their readiness high [1]. A larger agenda is not the same thing as being ready.

## Pressure is not a reduction plan

Pressure is the expectation that headcount will be squeezed. A reduction plan is a written list of who leaves, and when.

Seventy percent expect AI to create pressure to reduce the workforce in 2027 [1]. The same people also expect this:

| Expectation | Share |
|---|---|
| More task automation | 86 percent |
| New specialisms and roles | 82 percent |
| Current roles rewritten | 74 percent |
| Fewer entry-level and graduate roles | 39 percent |

One example. A count in the warehouse moves to a tool. A person checks the count. That is not the person leaving. That is the job changing. The 70 percent speaks to the first. The 74 percent speaks to the second. Reading both as “jobs will end” mixes the definitions.

The 39 percent is a separate sentence. The door in may narrow. Then where a young person learns must be written down [1].

## A working agent is not written authority

An agent is software that proposes a step in a person’s place. Authority is the name of the person who may accept that proposal.

Sixty-five percent use an agent or run a pilot. Twenty-eight percent have defined authority. Twenty-seven percent have a written policy [1].

A pilot that runs on a screen does not name the owner of the decision. The same gap shows up in hiring: [Workday, SAP and Metaview](/en/hr-ai-agents-workday-sap-metaview/).

## The gap is not the tool course

A skill gap is the distance between what the job needs and what the person knows.

Akan Abdula reported this order [1]:

| Gap | Share |
|---|---|
| Strategic thinking and problem solving | 64 percent |
| Leadership and people management | 58 percent |
| Data analytics | 47 percent |
| Change management | 41 percent |
| Artificial intelligence | 37 percent |

Spending the first gap’s budget on the fifth gap is defining the problem wrong. A tool course does not close a thinking gap.

## If experience is not on the table, the decision scatters

Forty-nine percent cite shifting priorities as a barrier. Forty-three percent cite weak ownership from senior management [1]. Employee experience is on 32 percent of CHRO lists and on 4 percent of the executive lists they report [1].

Yavuz Elkin does not call that indifference. Experience finds no seat when it is not tied to the cost of leaving or to productivity [1]. Eighty percent say pay and benefits costs have risen. Fifty-eight percent say employees’ financial wellbeing has not kept up [1].

The conclusion is plain. The tool was bought. The owner was not written down. Readiness stops there.

## Checklist

- [ ] Is the agent’s proposal on a different line from the person’s decision?
- [ ] Is the person who can change that decision named?
- [ ] Does the policy exist before the pilot opens? In this study, 27 percent had one [1].
- [ ] Does training start with strategic thinking, or with the tool?
- [ ] If entry roles shrink, is it written where a young person still learns?
- [ ] Is employee experience on the same page as the cost of leaving?

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people were in the study?

Ninety-two CHROs answered. Five CEOs and six CHROs were interviewed [1]. Most organizations have more than 1,000 employees. This is not the average of every employer.

### Does 70 percent mean layoffs?

No. Seventy percent expect pressure to reduce the workforce in 2027 [1]. Eighty-two percent expect new roles. Seventy-four percent expect roles to be rewritten. An expectation is not a published exit list.

### Is AI the largest gap?

Not here. Strategic thinking is first, at 64 percent. AI is fifth, at 37 percent [1].

### How many have written the authority?

Use or a pilot: 65 percent. Defined authority: 28 percent. A written policy: 27 percent [1].
