When we saw the number on Gallup's Türkiye page, we opened the table again to double-check: only 8% of employees in Türkiye are engaged at work [1]. That is 8 people in a team of 100. Around the same time, bad news came from the US too: engagement dropped to its lowest level since 2011 [2]. The question on every HR leader's mind is the same: what actually moves engagement now? Gallup's 2026 data on **employee engagement and AI** gives a clear hint. In teams using AI, the difference is not the tool itself but how the manager backs it [3]. This post brings together the Türkiye data, the AI–engagement link and a checklist you can use.

## What is employee engagement?

Gallup defines employee engagement as employees' involvement in and enthusiasm for their work and workplace [3]. It sorts employees into three groups: engaged, not engaged and actively disengaged [1].

In plain terms: people who own their work, people who are just "there," and people who have clearly checked out.

## Employee engagement in Türkiye: Gallup 2026 data

Gallup's State of the Global Workplace 2026 report was published in April 2026; country data are three-year rolling averages ending with data collected through December 2025 [1].

| Indicator | Türkiye | MENA region | Global |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engaged at work | 8% | 14% | 20% |
| Experienced a lot of stress the previous day | 66% | 48% | 40% |
| Experienced a lot of anger the previous day | 36% | 30% | 22% |
| Thriving in overall life | 19% | 26% | 34% |
| Say it is a good time to find a job where they live | 23% | 36% | 52% |

Source: Gallup, State of the Global Workplace 2026, Türkiye country-level data [1]. MENA: Middle East and North Africa.

The trend line is telling too:

| Three-year average ending | Engaged | Not engaged | Actively disengaged |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | 17% | 64% | 18% |
| 2020 | 14% | 61% | 25% |
| 2023 | 11% | 60% | 29% |
| 2024 | 10% | 65% | 25% |
| 2025 | 8% | 68% | 24% |

Source: Gallup [1]. Selected years from Gallup's annual series, compiled by us.

Quotable stat: **According to Gallup, only 8% of employees in Türkiye are engaged at work, far below the regional average of 14% and the global average of 20%** [1].

The second number that stood out to us is 23%. Only 23% of employees in Türkiye think it is a good time to find a job where they live [1]. That is fertile ground for the "checked out but can't leave" profile. Glassdoor chief economist Daniel Zhao describes the same dynamic in the US: in a low-hire market, workers feel stuck in their careers [4].

## The US hits a 15-year low

Gallup data published on October 28, 2026 show US engagement fell to 29% in the third quarter of 2026, the lowest since 2011 and down two points from the previous quarter [2]. The actively disengaged share rose to 18%, and 53% were not engaged [2].

The sharpest decline was among remote and hybrid employees in organizations that have adopted AI. Employees reported less clarity about what is expected of them and were less likely to feel someone at work cares about them as a person [2].

## Employee engagement and AI: does AI help?

Short answer: not on its own. Gallup's July 2026 analysis found that access to AI alone neither improves nor weakens the employee experience [3]. The conditions are what matter:

| Condition | Share engaged |
|---|---|
| Organization has a clear plan for integrating AI | 43% (no/don't know: 28%) |
| Uses AI frequently in their role | 39% (infrequent/non-users: 31%) |
| Manager actively supports the team's AI use | 48% (lower agreement: 30%) |
| All three conditions (in AI-adopting organizations) | 53% |

Source: Gallup, US employees, first half of 2026 [3].

Quotable stat: **Gallup finds 48% of employees whose manager actively supports AI use are engaged, compared with 30% of those without that support** [3].

By the third quarter, however, that advantage had narrowed considerably among remote and hybrid employees [2]. A plan and support that are announced once and then left alone lose their effect.

The rules are also shifting fast. Webrazzi, citing Bloomberg, reports that Apple cut some engineers' daily AI spending limit from $300 to $200 to control costs [5]. Changes like this can be perfectly reasonable, but if the reason and the expectation are not explained, the sense of "clarity" Gallup measures weakens again.

## Why are workers checking out?

The analysis Glassdoor's Daniel Zhao presented in November 2026 highlights five themes [4][6]:

- Feeling stuck in their careers
- Layoff anxiety rising faster than actual layoffs
- Rising burnout, with many people doing the jobs of several
- Skepticism of senior leadership, as Glassdoor leadership ratings fall
- AI uncertainty: job security is workers' number one AI concern

## How to improve employee engagement in the AI era: 7 steps

A roadmap built on Gallup's three priorities [2] and findings [3]:

1. **Clarify AI expectations by role.** People need to know how AI applies to their work, when to use it and how performance will be evaluated [2].
2. **Equip managers.** Gallup says active manager support has the strongest relationship with engagement among the AI-related factors studied [2].
3. **Prioritize remote and hybrid teams.** That is where the decline was sharpest [2], and where informal conversations are scarcest.
4. **Write the plan down and share it.** Engagement is 43% where there is a clear plan and 28% where there isn't [3].
5. **Track AI's impact alongside engagement.** Measure whether AI really improves productivity and problem-solving, and share good examples [2].
6. **Rebuild the "someone cares about me" feeling.** Gallup saw that item fall in Q3 [2]; a steady one-on-one calendar is the cheapest investment here.
7. **Make workload visible.** Zhao's burnout warning rests on whether the push for productivity is sustainable [4].

## A 90-day employee engagement checklist for HR

- [ ] Report the "I know what is expected of me" and manager support items from your last survey separately
- [ ] Analyze AI-using teams separately by remote/hybrid vs on-site [2]
- [ ] Write a one-page "where we use AI, where we don't" note for each job family [2]
- [ ] Give managers a short guide for AI conversations and a monthly team session to share examples [2]
- [ ] When AI usage rules or budgets change, explain why in writing the same day
- [ ] Track workload and overtime on the same dashboard as engagement data [4]
- [ ] After 90 days, ask the same questions again and take the difference to the leadership team

## Frequently asked questions

### What is employee engagement?

In Gallup's definition, it is employees' involvement in and enthusiasm for their work and workplace [3]. Gallup groups employees as engaged, not engaged and actively disengaged [1].

### What is the employee engagement rate in Türkiye?

According to Gallup's State of the Global Workplace 2026 report, 8% of employees in Türkiye are engaged, 2 points below the prior three-year average; the regional average is 14% and the global average 20% [1].

### How does AI affect employee engagement?

Access alone neither improves nor weakens it [3]. What matters is a clear plan, frequent use and especially manager support: 48% of employees whose manager actively supports AI use are engaged, versus 30% without that support [3].

### Why is US employee engagement falling?

Gallup reports a drop to 29% in Q3 2026, sharpest among remote and hybrid employees in AI-adopting organizations, with less clarity about expectations [2]. Glassdoor points to career stagnation, layoff anxiety, burnout, leadership skepticism and AI uncertainty [4].

### How is Gallup's global engagement data collected?

The global report is based on the Gallup World Poll, which typically surveys about 1,000 people per country face to face or by phone; country results are reported as three-year averages [1].
