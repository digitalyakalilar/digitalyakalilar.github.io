A message on WhatsApp or social media: "Work abroad, high pay, housing included, we handle the visa." Some of these are real opportunities. Others can end in far worse than lost money. This guide explains how an **overseas job offer scam** works, five signs to check before you accept and how to verify the offer through official channels.

## How big is the problem?

Job scams are growing worldwide. Reports of job scams to the US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) tripled between 2020 and 2024, and reported losses rose from $90 million to $501 million [1]. In the first half of 2025, reports of online job scams rose 19% compared with the same period a year earlier, with losses approaching $300 million [2]. These figures only count what was reported; the real scale is thought to be larger.

The most severe cases are in Southeast Asia. People lured to Myanmar, Cambodia and Laos with promises of high pay have reportedly been held in scam compounds and had their passports confiscated [3]. Türkiye's Ministry of Foreign Affairs has warned citizens about cases in Myanmar's border regions where people were tricked with online job offers, held and ransomed, and urged them not to trust such ads [4]. There are also reports of people taken from Türkiye to Cambodia with similar promises [5].

## 5 warning signs before you accept

### 1. You are asked to pay

An upfront fee for a visa, training, documents, a health report, "file processing" or a plane ticket. Genuine employers and licensed agencies do not ask candidates to pay to be hired this way. This is the clearest sign.

### 2. The company cannot be verified

No official website, no clear address, only a WhatsApp number or social media account. Or a well-known company's name is used, but contact runs through personal accounts instead of a corporate email.

### 3. Everything is too fast and too easy

You are "hired" after a short chat without being asked about your experience. There is no interview, or only messaging. You are pressured to decide: "Places are almost full."

### 4. The job and contract are vague

Duties, hours and pay are unclear; generic phrases like "customer service" or "online sales". There is no written contract, or you are told you will sign one on arrival. You are asked to travel on a tourist visa to work.

### 5. They ask for personal data and device access

Your passport, ID photo and bank details are requested at the very start. You are asked to install an app, give remote access to your phone or log in to an account. For account safety, see our [phone and account security guide](/en/phone-and-account-security/).

## How to verify the offer through official channels

- **Check the agency's licence.** In Türkiye, only İŞKUR and private employment agencies licensed by İŞKUR may act as intermediaries for jobs abroad. Licensed agencies are announced on İŞKUR's website and licences are valid for three years [6]. In other countries, check the equivalent national register.
- **Contact the consulate or embassy.** Türkiye's Foreign Ministry recommends researching the company thoroughly before accepting an offer and contacting embassies when needed [4].
- **Search the company in the destination country's official business register.** Check that the name and address match.
- **Confirm the work permit and visa type.** Travelling on a tourist visa to work puts you at legal risk.
- **Check travel advisories.** See whether your government has a current warning for the destination.

## What to do if you are suspicious

- Do not send money, share documents or click links.
- Keep screenshots of the messages.
- Report it to the police or prosecutor; if you are already abroad, contact your nearest embassy or consulate.
- Report the social media account that shared the ad to the platform.

## Checklist: Before you accept the offer

- [ ] Was I never asked for money at any stage?
- [ ] Does the company have an official website, address and corporate email?
- [ ] Is the agency on the official list of licensed recruitment agencies?
- [ ] Are duties, pay and working conditions clear in a written contract?
- [ ] Am I travelling with the right visa and work permit?
- [ ] Did I check the current travel advisory for the destination?
- [ ] Did I leave the company's name, address and contact details with someone close to me?

## Frequently asked questions

### How can I tell if an overseas job offer is real?

Verify the company and the agency through independent sources: the official list of licensed agencies (İŞKUR in Türkiye), the company's website and business register, and if needed your embassy in that country [4][6].

### Can an employer ask me to pay for my visa?

Being asked to pay upfront to be hired is the most common sign of a job scam. If you get such a request, verify the agency before you pay anything.

### Why are job offers in Myanmar and Cambodia risky?

People lured there by fake job ads have reportedly been held in scam compounds, and Türkiye's Foreign Ministry has warned about such cases in Myanmar [3][4].
