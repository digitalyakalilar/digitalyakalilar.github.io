Expecting more use is not the same as trusting the output. PwC published **Global Workforce Hopes and Fears Survey 2026** on 26 October 2026. About 50,000 workers took part worldwide. The Türkiye page does not state a separate sample size [1].

In Türkiye, 75 percent expect AI use in their work to rise over the next 12 months: 25 percent say it will rise, 31 percent say it will rise moderately, and 19 percent say it will rise significantly. The global figure is 60 percent [1]. Twenty-two percent in Türkiye say current use will largely stay the same, against 29 percent globally [1].

## Daily use is high. Agents are a separate line

Daily use of generative AI tools is 34 percent in Türkiye and 22 percent globally. Weekly use is 19 percent on both sides [1].

Daily use of AI agents is 16 percent in Türkiye and 10 percent globally. People who never use agents are 34 percent in Türkiye and 48 percent globally [1]. A generative tool and an agent are not the same product. Adding the two rates does not produce a new total.

## The barrier is not knowing the tool

In Türkiye the first barrier to more use is the accuracy and quality of the output, at 27 percent. Next are fear that AI replaces human work (25 percent), not enough opportunity at work (24 percent), and unclear company policy (21 percent) [1]. Lack of skill is 8 percent, against 15 percent globally [1].

Daily users are more selective. Globally, about a third of them, 31 percent, name accuracy or quality as the main barrier. That is 6 points above the global average [1]. People who use the tool often see the error more clearly.

Fear of replacement is also higher in Türkiye: 25 percent against 15 percent globally [1]. Over the next three years, 5 percent in Türkiye expect their role to disappear, against 3 percent globally [1]. The fear is larger than the share who expect the role itself to go.

## Two speeds in the same company

PwC splits the workforce into four groups. “Pioneers” are 14 percent. The “core workforce” is 56 percent [1]. Daily generative AI use is 51 percent among pioneers and 11 percent in the core workforce. Most of the core workforce say they have never used it [1].

One sentence, “the company uses AI,” hides that split. The small group that uses it and the majority that does not hear the same policy.

## Checklist: shadow use and company use

- [ ] Is it written where a generative tool is allowed, and where an agent is allowed?
- [ ] May candidate, customer, or employee data go into the tool?
- [ ] Which output needs a person to check it? Accuracy is the first barrier, at 27 percent [1].
- [ ] Can the person using the tool find the policy? Unclear policy is a barrier for 21 percent [1].
- [ ] Does the core workforce get a chance to use it, or does use stay with the pioneer group?
- [ ] Is “you will lose your job” being mixed up with the 5 percent who expect the role to disappear?

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people in Türkiye were surveyed?

PwC reports about 50,000 workers worldwide [1]. The 26 October 2026 page does not give a separate Türkiye count. Read the percentages as the Türkiye break, not as the whole country.

### Do 75 percent use AI today?

No. Seventy-five percent expect use to rise over the next 12 months [1]. Daily generative use is 34 percent. Daily agent use is 16 percent [1].

### Is lack of skill the main barrier?

Not in this break. Accuracy and quality lead, at 27 percent. Lack of skill is 8 percent [1].

### Why separate shadow use from company use?

A worker can bring a tool to the job. Unclear company policy is a barrier for 21 percent in Türkiye and 15 percent globally [1]. Without a policy, where the data goes is also unwritten.
