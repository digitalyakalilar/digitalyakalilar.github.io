The 2026 CHRO agenda: Readiness sits with the process owner, not the tool

In the Türkiye CHRO Agenda 2026 study, 70 percent of 92 HR leaders expect AI to pressure headcount in 2027. Most organizations piloting agents have not written down authority.

Buying a tool is not the same as being ready. In the Türkiye CHRO Agenda 2026 study by PERYÖN, FutureBright and Elkin Consultancy, 79 percent of CHROs say their agenda has grown. Only 5 percent rate their readiness as very high. Fifty percent rate it as high [1].

The study is an online survey of 92 CHROs, mostly at organizations with more than 1,000 employees, plus interviews with 5 CEOs and 6 CHROs. Results were published on 11 November 2026 [1]. The figures below are what those leaders said. They are not a forecast that every employer will follow the same path.

The 2027 expectation is pressure, not a single headcount plan

Seventy percent of CHROs expect AI to create pressure to reduce the workforce in 2027 [1]. The same group also expects:

Expectation Share
More automation of tasks 86 percent
New specialisms and roles 82 percent
Redesign of current roles 74 percent
Fewer entry-level and new-graduate roles 39 percent

Seventy percent does not mean “headcount will fall by a stated amount.” It means leaders expect pressure. The 39 percent figure is a separate expectation about the door into work for younger people [1]. Reading both as “jobs will disappear” drops the study’s own distinction.

Agents are in use. Authority is usually unwritten

Sixty-five percent of the organizations use AI agents or run a pilot. Twenty-eight percent have defined those agents’ responsibilities and authority. Twenty-seven percent have a written policy or guide [1].

Use is ahead of governance. A working pilot does not say who can change which decision. The same gap shows up in hiring agents: Workday, SAP and Metaview.

The largest skill gap is not AI

Akan Abdula of FutureBright reported this order [1]:

Skill gap CHROs
Strategic thinking and problem solving 64 percent
Leadership and people management 58 percent
Data analytics and literacy 47 percent
Change management 41 percent
Artificial intelligence 37 percent

Training that starts at fifth place leaves the first gap untouched.

Employee experience is on the CHRO list and rare on the executive list

Forty-nine percent of CHROs cite shifting priorities as a barrier. Forty-three percent cite weak ownership from senior management [1]. Employee experience is on 32 percent of CHRO agendas and on 4 percent of the executive priorities they report [1].

Yavuz Elkin of Elkin Consultancy does not read that as indifference. Employee experience struggles to hold a place at the table when it is not tied to productivity, the cost of turnover, or customer experience [1]. Eighty percent of CHROs say pay and benefits costs have risen. Fifty-eight percent say employees’ financial wellbeing has not moved with that rise [1].

Checklist before an agent pilot

  • Is the step the agent proposes separate from the decision a person makes?
  • Is the role that can change that decision named, with its authority?
  • Is there a written policy before the pilot opens? In this study, 27 percent had one [1].
  • Does the skills plan start with strategic thinking, or with the tool?
  • If entry-level roles may shrink, is there a plan for how early-career people still learn?
  • Is employee experience on the same page as turnover cost or productivity?

Frequently asked questions

How many people were in the study?

Ninety-two CHROs in the survey, plus interviews with 5 CEOs and 6 CHROs [1]. Most organizations have more than 1,000 employees. This is not an average of every employer in Türkiye.

Does 70 percent mean layoffs?

No. Seventy percent expect pressure to reduce the workforce in 2027 [1]. Eighty-two percent expect new roles, and 74 percent expect roles to be redesigned. An expected pressure is not an announced reduction plan.

Is AI the largest skill gap?

Not in this study. Strategic thinking and problem solving lead at 64 percent. AI is fifth, at 37 percent [1].

How many organizations have written agent authority?

Use or a pilot: 65 percent. Defined responsibility and authority: 28 percent. A written policy or guide: 27 percent [1].
