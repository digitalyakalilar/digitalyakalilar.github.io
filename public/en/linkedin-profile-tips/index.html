Optimizing your LinkedIn profile for modern hiring: An evidence-based guide

As skills-based hiring spreads, how is your LinkedIn profile found and evaluated? A step-by-step guide, from your photo and skills to verification and job scams, based on LinkedIn's own data and help pages.

HR TechOctober 13, 2026·8 min readLast updated: October 15, 2026
Share:XLinkedInWhatsAppFacebookTelegramEmail

Hiring teams increasingly find candidates through digital tools, and LinkedIn sits at the center of them. For you, the challenge is twofold: your profile needs to be visible to search and matching systems, and it needs to build trust when a human reads it.

  3. Staying visible: posting and engaging
  4. Protect your account, and yourself, from scams

One strong trend connects those two goals: skills-based hiring. According to LinkedIn Economic Graph's March 2025 report, an approach that evaluates candidates on whether they have at least half of a target job's top skills, rather than whether they've held the same title before, could expand talent pools by a median of 6.1x globally. The analysis covers 56 countries, including Türkiye [1]. LinkedIn's 2023 Skills-First report had estimated that this approach grows talent pools by nearly 10x on average [2].

The practical upshot: the more clearly your profile explains what you do and which skills you have, the more easily both algorithms and recruiters can place you. The steps below are based on LinkedIn's help pages and published data.

A note on the data: Some of LinkedIn's figures were published in different years using different methods. We've noted the year next to each statistic; treat older figures as directional rather than definitive.

How is your profile found?

LinkedIn says a complete profile increases your discoverability and your profile search appearances [3]. When your connections endorse the skills on your profile, it also increases the likelihood of you being discovered for opportunities related to those skills [3].

Signaling that you're looking also matters. When you use "Open to Work" to specify the types of jobs you're interested in and your preferred location, LinkedIn helps your profile show up in search results when recruiters look for suitable candidates [4].

Mind the privacy setting: You can share this only with recruiters who use LinkedIn Recruiter. LinkedIn says it takes steps to prevent Recruiter users at your current company from seeing it, but it can't guarantee complete privacy [4]. Keep that caveat in mind if you don't want your current employer to know.

Improving your profile, step by step

1. Add a professional photo

According to LinkedIn's current help page, members with a profile photo receive up to 2x more profile views and 3x more connection requests [5]. (A 2016 LinkedIn blog post put the profile-view figure at 21x; we suggest relying on the more conservative number on the current help page [6].)

How: Pick a recent photo where your face is clearly visible, against a simple background, with only you in it. LinkedIn's own advice is to use a good-quality photo that doesn't include your pet or partner [6].

2. Don't let your headline be just a job title

LinkedIn notes that your headline is created when you add your positions, but that you can rewrite it to promote an area of expertise [3].

Formula: Role + area of expertise + tools/methods

  • Weak: "Specialist"
  • Strong: "HR Business Partner | Talent Management & Performance Systems | SAP SuccessFactors"
  • Strong: "Finance Specialist | Budget Planning & Reporting | Excel & Power BI"

Use the terms that appear most often in the job postings you're targeting; recruiters will most likely search with those same terms.

3. Keep your About section short and focused

LinkedIn recommends that your About section express your mission, motivation and skills, ideally in one or two paragraphs; bullet points are fine if you're not comfortable writing paragraphs [3]. According to a 2016 LinkedIn post, a summary of 40 words or more made it more likely to show up in search [6].

Template:

  1. What do you do, and who do you create value for? (1–2 sentences)
  2. Concrete results: 2–3 achievements at most, with numbers where possible
  3. Your areas of expertise and the tools you use (keywords)
  4. What you're focused on now, or what kind of opportunities you're open to

4. Keep your experience current and results-focused

LinkedIn's 2016 data showed that members with up-to-date positions were discovered up to 18x more in searches [6]. Make sure each position has an accurate title, company and date range. LinkedIn also suggests adding media samples to showcase your work [3].

Write results, not duties:

  • Duty: "Responsible for preparing monthly reports."
  • Result: "Automated the monthly reporting process, cutting preparation time from 3 days to 1."

Only share figures you can stand behind and that don't breach any confidentiality obligations.

5. Build your skills list strategically

This is the most important section for skills-based hiring. LinkedIn's 2020 data showed that 87% of hirers consider the skills a candidate lists crucial when vetting them, and that members with 5 or more skills on their profile are discovered up to 27x more in recruiter searches [7].

AI skills stand out in particular. According to Microsoft and LinkedIn's 2024 Work Trend Index, 66% of leaders say they wouldn't hire someone without AI skills, and LinkedIn saw a 142x increase in members adding AI skills such as ChatGPT and Copilot to their profiles [8].

How:

  1. Open 3–5 job postings you're targeting and list the skills that come up most.
  2. Add the skills you genuinely have; combine hard and soft skills [7].
  3. Move the skills you most want to be known for to the top. LinkedIn targets endorsement requests toward the skills you prioritize [6].
  4. Add the AI tools you use and the concrete work you do with them.

6. Set your location correctly

LinkedIn's 2016 data showed that more than 30% of recruiters used location in advanced search [6]. Enter both your city and, in your Open to Work settings, your remote or hybrid preferences accurately [4].

7. Ask for recommendations

There's no limit to how many recommendations you can request on LinkedIn; once you accept one, it's visible to your network by default, and you can hide any you don't feel suit your goals [3].

How to ask: Instead of a generic request, remind the person of a specific project you worked on together: "Could you briefly describe my coordination role in last year's ERP migration?" It makes their job easier and produces a more concrete recommendation.

8. Verify your profile

According to LinkedIn, verified members see 60% more profile views and 50% more engagement on average. Identity, workplace and education verification are free and optional. In many countries, identity verification is available through Persona with an NFC-enabled passport; workplace verification is possible at supported companies through methods such as a work email [9]. Check in Settings which options are available to you.

9. Create a custom profile URL

LinkedIn recommends creating a personal URL so your public profile shows up better in search engine results [3]. A clean address like "linkedin.com/in/firstnamelastname" instead of "linkedin.com/in/firstname-lastname-123abc" also looks more professional on your CV and email signature. In your public profile settings you can choose which sections appear to search engines [5].

Staying visible: posting and engaging

Your profile isn't a document you edit once and forget. LinkedIn describes it as your "personal storyboard", the place where people find you and keep up with your activity [3]. Keeping it alive doesn't take a big campaign:

  • Short posts that show your expertise: a lesson from a project you just finished, your three key takeaways from an industry report, or a practical answer to a question you get asked often.
  • Meaningful comments: on posts from experts you follow, a comment that adds your own experience (rather than "Agreed") brings new visitors to your profile.
  • Confidentiality limits: check your company's social media policy before sharing internal figures, client names or unannounced projects.

We couldn't find current, general-purpose data published by LinkedIn on which days and times work best for posting. Look at your own analytics and experiment to find when your followers are most active.

Protect your account, and yourself, from scams

The more visible your profile, the more likely you are to encounter fake job offers. According to LinkedIn's Job Search Safety Pulse published in May 2026, 90% of reported scam messages involve an attempt to move the conversation off LinkedIn to private messaging apps. The red flags surveyed professionals recognized most were early requests for sensitive information, requests for upfront payments, and pressure to act quickly [11].

Do:

  • Keep early conversations on LinkedIn, and check whether the job also appears on the company's own careers site [11].
  • Review the recruiter's profile and any verification badge [11].
  • If someone asks for ID documents, bank details or a "training fee", stop and report it.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication for your account: Settings & Privacy > Sign in & security > Two-factor authentication. LinkedIn offers an authenticator app or SMS [10].

A 15-minute action plan

Time Step
2 min Check or update your photo
3 min Rewrite your headline using the formula
4 min Update your About section with the template
3 min Add 5+ skills and move your top three to the top
1 min Check your location and Open to Work visibility
2 min Turn on two-factor authentication and ask one person for a recommendation

Don't edit your profile once and leave it. Update it whenever you finish a new project, earn a certification or learn a new tool. As skills-based hiring spreads, a current, clear profile will remain one of your most tangible career assets.
