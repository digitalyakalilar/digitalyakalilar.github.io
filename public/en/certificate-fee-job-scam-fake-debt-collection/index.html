A job offer with a "certificate requirement": The document fee and fake debt collection scam

Scammers in Turkey call job seekers about hospital jobs, demand a 5,000-6,000 lira "document fee" for a required certificate, then send fake debt collection threats to those who refuse. How it works and how to protect yourself.

The phone rings: "There's a job at a hospital, good pay. Just one condition: a certificate." Soon a "document fee" follows. People who refuse to pay get messages saying debt collection proceedings will start. This guide explains how the certificate fee job scam works and how to protect yourself.

How the trap works

According to Turkish news reports, job seekers are called from different numbers or reached through social media ads. They are promised well-paid jobs at public or private hospitals. During the call, a certificate in hygiene, patient admission or security is said to be mandatory, and a "document fee" of 5,000 to 6,000 lira is requested [1]. Links sent over WhatsApp are also used to collect personal data [1].

Those who don't pay receive messages saying a document was issued in their name and debt collection (icra) will begin if they don't pay [1]. A user complaint on a Turkish complaints platform describes a guaranteed placement "through İŞKUR" (Turkey's public employment agency), an electronic contract allegedly created without consent, a 5,500 lira "cancellation fee" for a service never received, and SMS threats of debt collection [2]. That is one user's complaint, not an official finding, but it shows the same playbook.

3 things to know

1. Real employers don't charge you to hire you

Document, certificate, file, training or registration fee: whatever the name, an upfront payment for being hired is the clearest red flag. İŞKUR does not ask job seekers for upfront money either [1].

2. No signed contract, no legal basis for the threat

As the report notes, without a signed contract or official proceeding, these debt collection messages have no legal standing [1]. In Turkey, a real enforcement file shows up in the e-Devlet (e-Government) portal, so check it there yourself instead of trusting a link or number in the SMS.

3. Don't click links or share your ID number and codes

WhatsApp links, national ID numbers, addresses and one-time SMS codes are what scammers are really after [1][2]. Sharing a code can let someone sign a contract or make a transaction in your name. See our phone and account security guide to protect your accounts.

One step further: becoming part of the crime

Some fake job offers don't ask for money but use your bank account instead. You are asked to forward money that lands in your account to other accounts. In a Hürriyet report, an IT expert warns that the job seeker may then face a criminal investigation [3]. Never accept an "easy job, just transfers" offer.

If you already paid

  • Call your bank through its official channel right away, report the transfer and ask to stop or dispute it [1][3].
  • Keep call logs, the IBAN, messages and screenshots of threatening SMS.
  • Report it to the police, gendarmerie or public prosecutor; in Turkey you can also use CİMER [1].
  • Block the numbers and report the account that posted the ad.

For similar traps, see our ghost job guide and overseas job offer scam guide.

FAQ

Do İŞKUR or hospitals charge a fee to hire?

No. İŞKUR does not ask job seekers for upfront money [1], and a real employer does not charge candidates a document or certificate fee.

If I don't pay, will debt collection really start?

Without a contract you signed or an official proceeding, these messages have no legal basis [1]. Check your enforcement files in e-Devlet to be sure.
