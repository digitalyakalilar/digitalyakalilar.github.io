What are ghost jobs? Around one in five job postings may never be filled

Greenhouse found that 18–22% of job postings on its platform were ghost jobs that companies never planned to fill. How to spot ghost jobs, why companies post them and how to protect your application time.

If you have sent dozens of applications and heard nothing back, the problem may not be you. The hiring software company Greenhouse found that 18–22% of postings on its platform were roles the company was not actually trying to fill [1][2]. This guide explains what ghost jobs are, why companies post them and how to spot ghost jobs before you spend time applying.

What is a ghost job?

A ghost job is a posting that stays live even though the company has no real plan to hire for it. It is different from a scam: the company is real, and the ad runs on its own site or official accounts. But your application never turns into a job.

Ghost jobs in numbers

Indicator Finding Source
Share of ghost postings 18–22% of postings Greenhouse [1][2]
Companies posting at least one ghost job At least 70% (Q2 2024) Greenhouse [1]
Companies posting ghost jobs regularly 15% Greenhouse [1]
Highest-rate industries Construction 38%, art 34%, legal 29% Greenhouse [1]
Candidates who think they saw a fake listing 3 in 5 (2,500+ workers) Greenhouse [2]

Two caveats: these figures reflect Greenhouse's own client base, not the whole job market. The coverage is based on 2024 data and was published in January 2025 [1][2]. We found no comparable measurement for Türkiye.

Why do companies post ghost jobs?

According to a Resume Builder survey of hiring managers, the most common reasons are [1]:

  • To look open to outside talent (67%)
  • To look like the company is growing (66%)
  • To make current employees feel replaceable

Other reasons include forgetting to close the ad, collecting a candidate pool before budget approval, or formally posting a role that already has an internal candidate.

How to spot ghost jobs: 6 signs

No single sign proves a posting is fake; be careful when several appear together.

  1. The ad has been up for months. The same posting stays live for a long time or keeps getting reposted.
  2. The description is vague. No duties, team, manager or salary range; only generic phrases.
  3. It is not on the company's careers page. The ad appears on a job board but not on the company's own site, or the other way round.
  4. The company is in the news for layoffs. If it is cutting staff at the same time, question the posting.
  5. There is no response at all. Your application sits "under review" for months.
  6. There is no verification badge. Greenhouse and LinkedIn now offer verification features that help candidates filter out postings that may waste their time [1]. A badge is no guarantee, but its absence is a question mark.

How to protect your application time

  • Prioritise postings from the last 2–3 weeks.
  • Confirm the posting on the company's careers page.
  • Send a short LinkedIn message to the recruiter or hiring manager. "Is this role still open?" is often the fastest answer.
  • Track your applications in a spreadsheet. Date, channel, response. A company that keeps reopening the same role shows up there.
  • Aim, don't spray. Apply to fewer postings you are sure are real, with a CV tailored to each. To make your CV readable at first glance, see our guide on how long recruiters look at a resume.

A ghost job only wastes your time; a fake job offer can cost you money and personal data. For the difference, see our guide to fake job offers from abroad.

Checklist: Before you apply

  • Was the posting published in the last 30 days?
  • Is it also on the company's own careers page?
  • Does it list duties, team and, ideally, a salary range?
  • Has the company been in the news for layoffs recently?
  • Does the posting have a verification badge?
  • Have you confirmed with the recruiter that the role is open?

Frequently asked questions

Are ghost jobs illegal?

A ghost job is a posting from a real company that does not plan to fill the role; it is different from scams that ask for money or personal data. The legal position varies by country. This article is not legal advice.

How common are ghost jobs?

According to Greenhouse, 18–22% of postings on its platform are ghost jobs; in construction the rate reaches 38% [1][2].

How can I tell if a job posting is still active?

Check the posting date, look for the ad on the company's careers page and ask the recruiter directly.
