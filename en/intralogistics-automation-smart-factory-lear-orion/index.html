Intralogistics automation: inside Lear's Orion smart factory blueprint, with market data and a starter checklist

Seating supplier Lear is presenting its Orion plant, opening in early 2027, as the blueprint for its global network, targeting a 10-15% cut in conversion costs. What intralogistics means, the technologies at Orion, market data, the Türkiye link and a checklist.

Industry 4.0 & ManufacturingNovember 10, 2026·6 min readLast updated: November 10, 2026
Share:XLinkedInWhatsAppFacebookTelegramEmail

We tried to picture the flow Lear describes. A piece of seat foam arrives at the dock. It goes into an automated store, gets called to the line, leaves assembly as a seat, is tested and rolls onto an automatically loaded trailer. Lear CEO Ray Scott describes the new Orion plant in Michigan in roughly those terms: a layer that ties together inventory, material management, storage and real-time decision-making from the moment a component arrives at the dock to the moment a finished seat leaves the facility [1]. He calls it integrated intralogistics. If you want a concrete picture of intralogistics automation, this plant is one of the best current examples. Below we cover the definition, the technologies at Orion, the market data and a starter checklist for manufacturers.

What is intralogistics?

The German engineering federation VDMA defines intralogistics as the organization, control, execution and optimization of in-house material flows, information flows and goods handling in industry, retail and public institutions [4].

So it is not the logistics outside the plant (transport, distribution) but the flow inside the walls: receiving, storage, line feeding, buffer stock and shipping preparation. All the hardware and software that run that flow fall under intralogistics.

Lear Orion: a smart factory example

When Lear reported third-quarter results on October 30, 2026, it said its complete seat facility in Orion, Michigan, is on track to launch early next year, calling it the company's "most sophisticated plant" and a showcase for its IDEA by Lear technology roadmap [2]. According to Manufacturing Dive, the plant will build seating for three General Motors models, and Lear intends to scale the model to other programs such as the Ford Super Duty [1].

The technologies listed for Orion:

Area Technology at Orion
Storage Two separate automated storage and retrieval systems (AS/RS): one for raw components such as foam, one for finished goods
Storage A four-directional storage system
Material movement An overhead suspended material storage and delivery system
Material movement Automated guided vehicles (AGVs) and autonomous mobile robots (AMRs)
Shipping Automated trailer loading
Production Smart ovens
Quality Automated end-of-line testing and AI vision inspection
Workforce management Cycle time deviation and dynamic line scheduling tools

Source: Manufacturing Dive, from Lear's October 30, 2026 earnings call [1].

Quotable stat: Lear expects automation at Orion to reduce conversion cost requirements by 10% to 15% [1].

Why the "integrated intralogistics" layer matters

Taken one by one, nothing on that list is new. AS/RS and AGVs have been around for decades. The difference is wiring them all into a single decision layer. In Scott's words: "We are moving towards automated execution, meaning our people are focused on solving problems, not performing routine tasks" [1].

The second point is ownership. Lear built its own technology through acquisitions such as WIP Industrial Automation, Thagora Technology and InTouch Automation, and both AS/RS were developed by an internal team that came from its purchase of ASI Automation [1]. As Scott put it: "We own the IP, we control the road map, and we can scale it across our global footprint" [1].

Not every manufacturer can take that route. But the question is the same for everyone: are you buying systems one at a time, or also designing the data flow between them?

What the market says

VDMA's February 2026 market report paints a cautious picture [4]:

Indicator Value
German materials handling and intralogistics production, 2025 €25.8 billion (−7%)
Germany outlook, 2026 Stagnation at roughly the same level
EU27 production, 2024 An estimated €65.7 billion (about +3%)
Global export volume, 2024 €123.5 billion (+1%)

Source: VDMA Materials Handling and Intralogistics, February 2026 [4].

VDMA says that despite the short-term cooling, automation, robotics, digitalization and AI-based logistics processes remain long-term growth drivers, with a recovery possible from 2027 [4]. Consolidation continues too: AutoStore said it will buy Dutch company Logiqs, which develops roaming robots for pallet-sized loads, for about $33 million [5].

The Türkiye connection

This is not a distant story for Türkiye. Three data points:

  • Lear has a company in Türkiye registered with the Bursa Trade Registry in 2001 (Lear Otomotiv Sanayi ve Ticaret Ltd. Şti.) [3].
  • According to OİB data, Türkiye's automotive supply industry exported $1.422 billion in October 2026, the sector's largest product group, and $13.556 billion in January-October. Germany is its top market [6].
  • TÜİK data released on November 10, 2026 show manufacturing output rose 2.6% year on year and 2.3% month on month in September [7].

Costs are tight as well: OİB chairman Kemal Yazıcı says the sector's costs have more than doubled in euro terms over the past four years [6]. A plant like Orion sharpens the question of who can build the same seat at a lower conversion cost. For Türkiye's robot density figures, see our post on industrial robot statistics.

Intralogistics automation starter checklist

The list below is our own recommendation, drawn from the Orion example in the sources and the VDMA definition:

  • Map material and information flows from receiving to shipping on one diagram (the two flows in the VDMA definition) [4]
  • Treat raw material and finished goods stock separately; Orion uses a separate AS/RS for each [1]
  • Decide which moves are fixed-route (AGV) and which need flexibility (AMR) [1]
  • Design the data model that will connect every system to one inventory and decision layer from day one [1]
  • Decide whether quality checks sit at end of line or in process; Orion uses end-of-line testing and AI vision inspection [1]
  • Take a conversion cost baseline now; Lear's 10-15% target only means something against a baseline [1]
  • Prepare a training plan that redefines roles around problem-solving rather than routine tasks [1]
  • Buy or build? Make IP ownership and roadmap control part of the decision [1]

Frequently asked questions

What does intralogistics mean?

In VDMA's definition, it is the organization, control, execution and optimization of in-house material flows, information flows and goods handling in industry, retail and public institutions [4].

What is the difference between intralogistics and logistics?

Logistics also covers moving goods between companies and locations. Intralogistics, by definition, focuses on the flow inside a facility: receiving, storage, line feeding and shipping preparation [4].

What are some smart factory examples?

Lear's Orion plant, opening in early 2027, is a current one: two AS/RS, AGVs and AMRs, automated trailer loading, end-of-line testing and AI vision inspection are joined in a single "integrated intralogistics" layer [1][2].

How much can factory automation cut costs?

It depends on the plant. Lear expects automation at Orion to reduce conversion cost requirements by 10% to 15% [1].

Is the intralogistics market growing?

According to VDMA, German production fell 7% in 2025 and stagnation is expected in 2026, but automation, robotics and AI remain long-term growth drivers, with a recovery possible from 2027 [4].
